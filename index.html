NYC Street Tree Care 101

You want to care for street trees in NYC, but you’re not sure where to begin? I’m Zarina and I’m one of the volunteer Care Captains with they NYC Parks stewarship program. This page contains steps and resources on basic street tree care as recommended by the NYC Parks.

Tools

These are a few basic gardening tools that you can use for street tree care activities. The handheld tools are small and relatively inexpensive. Most importantly, they don’t take up too much space for storage.

Gardening gloves: must have
Always wear gloves, specifically gardening gloves, to protect your hands while you tend the tree bed.

Hand cultivator: must have
It is used to break up compacted top layer soil in the tree pit, lose and aerate the soil, remove weeds, and mix compost in the soil.

Hand trowel: must have
Use the trowel to dig, remove weeds, debris and pieces of trash, scoop and mix compost in the soil. If a task requires digging and scooping, a trowel is the best tool for it.

Broom: must have
A regular broom, or a hand broom is for sidewalk sweeping and tidying, because tree care tasks can get quite messy.

Weed puller: nice to have
A weed puller is very useful to remove very stubborn weeds from the ground, and extract the deep roots to prevent regrowth. You can use a trowel instead if you don’t have this.

Hand rake: nice to have
Use the hand rake to spread and adjust mulch layer evenly in the tree bed. A cultivator works well to for this purpose.

Other tools and supplies:

  • Trash picker: to remove litter, debris, and hazardous items from the ground around trees
  • Trash bags
  • Watering can or hose: if you want to water the tree
  • Gardening shovel: not necessary. If you have one readily available, it’s useful for heavy digging and scooping soil and compost.

Basic street tree care

Here are five steps to care for a street tree that hasn’t been tended to before. I strongly recommend reading the NYC Parks street tree care guidelines linked below. These steps will help you get started, but attending an in-person street tree care event offered by NYC Parks or Big Reuse is a great way to gain hands-on experience and learn proper care techniques.

1. Remove trash and debris from the tree bed
Read the trash cleanup guidelines from NYC Parks.
Wear gloves and use trash picker! Remove everything: litter, large pieces of cement, animal waste, cigarette butts.

2. Remove weeds
Read the weed removal guidelines from NYC Parks.
Weeds can be composted, so separate them from trash and compost accordingly.

3. Loosen and aerate the top layer soil
Over time, the top layer soil in the tree bed gets compacted. To aerate the soil of your tree bed, use a hand cultivator and gently rake the dirt 1-inch to 3-inches down, and fluff it. This will break up the compacted soil and allow more oxygen to get down to the roots. Be careful not to damage the tree roots!

4. Add compost and mulch
Read the mulch and soil amendment guidelines from NYC Parks, especially about the compost and mulch “ring” to prevent root rot.
Compost is good to add to the soil, but it’s not necessary.

5. Continuing maintenance

  • Clean and sweep the area around the tree bed when you’re done.
  • After your initial cleanup, check the tree bed every 1 to 2 weeks. Pick up litter around the tree, and pull weeds before they get large.
  • Watering the tree is important, but please read the tree watering guidelines from NYC Parks.
  • Check the mulch layer at least once a year. If it has thinned to less than 2 or 3 inches, add more mulch to restore the depth.

Care Captain program

The street trees in NYC are under the management of NYC Parks, and the department encourages volunteer to care for them. Attending an in-person street tree care event offered by NYC Parks is a great way to gain hands-on experience and learn proper care techniques.

You can also be a volunteer Care Captain (Super Steward) with NYC Parks. To become a Care Captain, you need to:

  1. Attend an in-person street tree care event offered by NYC Parks for practical training
  2. Attend a Care Captain webinar training for everything about NYC urban forest

More information on street tree stewardship:

  • NYC Parks stewardship info and events (basic)
  • NYC Parks advanced stewardship info and events — how this is different from basic stewardship

Note: You don’t need to be a Care Captain to perform basic street tree care.
